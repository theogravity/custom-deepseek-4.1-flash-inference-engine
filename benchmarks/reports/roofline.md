# Estimated decode ceiling on the two RTX PRO 6000s

The bandwidth measurements and derived ceilings apply to the owner's benchmark profile on both inference cards, including a 375 W per-card power limit, +450 MHz core and +4,000 MHz VRAM P0 offsets. [Benchmark configuration](../results/workstation/benchmark-gpu-settings.json)

Repeating the bandwidth probe at the 600 W GPU limit measured 1,702.71 and 1,703.12 GB/s. With the same packed weights, that gives a 6.478 ms sequential weight-read floor, or 154.37 target-only tok/s. The GPU copy test measured memory bandwidth directly. The host CPU has a separate socket power limit. [600 W bandwidth](../results/workstation/hardware-600w.json) · [600 W packed ceiling](../results/workstation/roofline-packed-600w.json) · [CPU power readback](../results/workstation/benchmark-cpu-power.json)

Single-stream, one-token-at-a-time decode for the packed target needs approximately 11.03 GB of selected weights per token at short context. Measured VRAM copy bandwidth was 1,702.39 and 1,702.74 GB/s with both cards active at 375 W. With 20 layers per stage and sequential stage execution, the weight-read floor is 6.48 ms, or **154 tok/s**. With the old BF16-expanded dense weights it was 10.47 ms, or **95.6 tok/s**. The ideal limit if every byte could be perfectly split across both cards at the same time is approximately **309 tok/s** for the packed representation. The deployed staged expert split parallelizes part of each layer. These are idealized weight-traffic ceilings assuming resident experts, without KV reads, arithmetic, Engram misses, PCIe traffic or scheduling delays. Speculation changes the denominator. One verification pass reads dense weights for an entire group of proposed tokens. Routed expert traffic grows with the number of distinct selected experts in that group. An Nsight trace averaged about 23 expert selections per layer after grouping, and each unique expert costs roughly 13.3 MB of packed weights. The resulting expert traffic was about 13 GB per verification round, across the 40 target and three draft layers. The unsharded trace spent 9.82 ms in expert projections and 5.55 ms in packed dense projections per 22.97 ms round. The deployed version splits target expert work across both cards. Effective output throughput is accepted tokens plus the target-supplied token, divided by total time for drafting and verification. It varies with the continuation, draft length, residency, and context. A single theoretical speculative rate would require specifying these quantities.
