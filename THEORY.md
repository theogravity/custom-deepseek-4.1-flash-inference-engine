# Estimated single-stream decode ceiling

At short context the Rust engine reads approximately 8.90 GB for its first 20 layers and 8.92 GB for its remaining layers and vocabulary head. The stages execute consecutively for one token. Simultaneous device-copy tests on this workstation measured 1,702.39 GB/s and 1,702.74 GB/s on the two RTX PRO 6000s at their 375 W limits. This gives a 10.47 ms weight-read floor, or **95.6 output tokens/s**, if every selected expert is already in VRAM. The calculation assumes one read per weight and no other delays. It excludes PCIe transfers, KV reads, Engram page faults, kernel launches and arithmetic. The CUDA expert kernel can reread packed tiles and its trellis decoding costs instructions. [Calculated bytes](results/roofline.json) · [Calculator](tools/roofline.py)

For the model and hardware in general, balancing the work across both GPUs concurrently and reading the original quantized weights directly gives a different estimate. Using a BF16 output head gives 3,404.78 GB/s divided by 11.719 GB per token, or **291 tokens/s**. Quantizing that head to FP8 gives 307 tokens/s under the same assumptions. These figures assume ordinary one-token decoding. A speculative engine's throughput depends on the number of draft tokens the target accepts and the extra work required to verify them.
