At the 600 W GPU setting, one client generating 128 verified receipt tokens exceeded 300 output tok/s across all eight prompt lengths. With 524,160 input tokens it reached 322.9 tok/s direct and 322.0 via LiteLLM on OpenAI Chat. On the upstream Python edit, the native service reached 278 tok/s at C1 and 459 tok/s aggregate at C6. ## Six concurrent agents

Six independent coding requests each generated 256 tokens. Across the six warm waves, aggregate decode measured **395.5 tok/s** and median per-stream decode was **84.7 tok/s**. The overlap and target-pass counters showed genuine simultaneous decoding, and all 42 outputs across the warm and first waves matched the serial controls byte for byte. A separate single-client test of the six-slot scheduler through LiteLLM measured 330.5 tok/s across 18 warm new-code samples. [Concurrent coding](../results/concurrency/six-coding.jsonl) · [Serial controls](../results/concurrency/six-serial-control.jsonl) · [Single-client proxy](../results/clients/six-slot-single-client.jsonl)

Six requests with a restored common document and distinct late receipts each consumed **524,160 input + 128 output = 524,288 tokens**. Every wave had 18 successful six-request target passes. Each request returned its own correct receipts. The six warm waves had a median aggregate decode rate of **361.1 tok/s**, a median individual stream rate of **60.9 tok/s**, and a median end-to-end wall time of **5.36 s** for the wave, including cache restore. The first wave in the freshly started process took 13.53 s. The aggregate rate including restore was 143.4 tok/s across warm waves. [Full-window streams and timings](../results/concurrency/six-full-restored.jsonl)

In the deployed offload and context-validation build, the first full-window stream started returning text after a median **1.11 s**, down from 3.22 s in the earlier run. The other five began later as their prefixes restored. This wider delivery interval brought the first-text-to-last-text aggregate to **177.0 tok/s**. Median per-stream delivery was **61.2 tok/s**, the wave took **5.43 s** including restore, and every output matched the earlier build. Six short coding clients delivered **394.4 tok/s** aggregate on this release. Aggregate delivery from the first visible text to the last measured 100.0 tok/s; five of the six streams individually delivered 52–54 tok/s. ## KV restore and rotation measurements

Eight requests reached 524,288 tokens apiece while sharing six GPU slots. Six resumed independent prior conversations, and two used a common document with distinct late receipts. We set the live RAM tier to 1 GiB and the rotation quantum to four steps to exercise disk overflow. The first wave finished in 44.04 s and the second in 39.63 s. All 16 responses matched their individual serial replays. Across the two concurrent waves the engine saved and restored 14 live states through RAM and 27 through disk, with zero save or restore failures. ## Choosing between the learned and source drafters

When DSpark is ready, the scheduler uses it if fewer than 16 consecutive tokens agree with a source occurrence. With 16–31 matches it can propose the next 16 source tokens, with 32–63 it can propose 64, and with at least 64 it can propose the configured maximum. The target verifies every proposal. The Python rewrite used 25,598 input tokens and reached its 512-output-token limit in both modes. It measured 166.2 tok/s with fixed 128-token lookup and 308.1 tok/s after this change, with identical generated text. The complete Go edit measured 866.5 tok/s and passed the same tests.
